# Dotburg systems

How each system works: numbers, what is saved, what was measured, test flags. One section per system; read only the ones a task touches and update a section in the same change that alters its system. The design and the why are in `vision.md`; the file each system lives in is in `file-map.md`. Moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-13 so that file stays short.

## Fires (`Fires.cs`, v0.46, RimWorld style)
- A `Fire` sits on one lot with a `Size` (0..1, how fierce) and `Burn` (0..1, how much of the building is gone). Once a second (`StepFires`): size grows logistically on fuel (`0.2 * flam * s * (1 - s)`, `Fuel` table: flammability and how long the building lasts); it is cut by rain (`rainAmt`), snow cover, a well or fountain next door (bucket chain, 0.015), a fire brigade from Town tier (0.02) and each dot-man at the blaze (0.01). Burn grows with size; at 1 the building is a ruin. A fierce fire (size over 0.4) can jump to a lot one block away: chance `0.012 * size * neighbour flammability * dryness * (1 - rain)`. Stone buildings (church, cathedral, tower) resist; well and fountain never burn; a park burns out but stays.
- Firefighting: `SendFighters` sends up to 6 (`Headcount / 6`, at least 2) of the nearest free dot-men to the door (`Goal.Fight`, a blue bucket pixel); at night 40% of sleepers wake to the alarm. Sleepers and the bedridden inside wake at 22% a step; if still inside when size passes 0.5, 3% a step die (rare). People never enter a burning lot (`Burning`).
- Ignition: `FireDice` (kitchen or oven fire, about once per 50 days in a 20-building town, more in summer, dry spells (`dryDays`) and for bakeries, inns and mills, never in rain); `Strike` (a lightning flash may hit a building, taller ones likelier; rain nearly always puts it out within seconds); `Arson` (a saboteur, bigger start). At most 6 fires and ruins up to 30% of the lots (`RuinCap`).
- Aftermath (`BurnDown`): the lot becomes `ruin` and a `Ruin` record (what it was, `At`) is kept; gold -4, mood down, ruins and fires weigh on mood. Harsh rule (0.8+) blames a saboteur and arrests (40%); commune or council collect (50%). `RebuildRuins` rebuilds through scaffolding after 1 to 2 days (guild half, poor town 1.6x, rich 0.7x). Ruins are saved (`Ruins` in the save; an older save's ruin without a record rebuilds as a house after a day). Fires in progress are not saved.
- Measured (`--soak 10 --units 150 --fires`, 60 fires each, clear weather): a small town (50 units) loses the building 78% of the time, a mid one (90) 53%, a grown one with a brigade (150) 5%; in rain none, out in about 2 s. Lives lost: none in 60 daytime fires. Soak output lists `Fire!`, `burns down`, spread and rain lines.
- Test flags: `--fire` (screenshot with a fire going), `--soak N --units M --fires` (the table above).

## War and blight (`War.cs`, v0.47, 2026-11-11)
- **War with a sister** (`WarOn`, `MaybeWar`, `StartWar`, `StepWar`): rolled with the events, rate per day `4 * (0.002 + 0.015 * harshness + 0.004 * harsh-sisters)`, at least 5 days after the last war, town over 10. A foe is picked by how harsh it is (and how unlike us). Up to 6 soldiers (12% of the town, never below 4 left, never the leader, children or the sick) walk out of the west gate (`Goal.Leave`, `To = "#war"`, so no sister counts them). The war lasts 1 to 3 days: news from the front, a soldier lost (40% of news), and a raid (`Calm` and 0.35 a day) that calls `Arson`. At the end: odds `0.5 + 0.2 * (our harshness - theirs) + 0.005 * (our headcount - theirs)`, clamped 0.15 to 0.85. Victory: the foe loses a fifth, plunder +5 gold, mood up, a festival, the foe may change rule (40%). Defeat: growth pool lost, -6 gold, mood -0.15, aid asked, then 30% revolution under harsh rule or 20% army takeover under gentle rule. Survivors walk back in (`ReturnSoldiers`, `SettleColour`).
- **While at war:** -2 gold per check, mood -0.06 unless the rule is 0.8+ harsh, no envoys, fairs or caravans, no aid from the foe. Soldiers already past the gate are saved as dot-men at home (`AwayColours`); the war itself is not saved.
- **War between two sisters** (`SisterWarOn`): harsh (0.5+) sisters, rate 0.024 a day each; 1 to 3 days; the loser loses a fifth, then absorbed (10%, if smaller and a fate is ready) or changes rule (40%), and half the time her people reach this gate as refugees (`Refugees`, shared with `SisterRevolt`).
- **Blight** (`BlightOn`, `StepBlight`): one plague or famine at a time, starts in a random town of the region when `Calm` (rate 0.025 a day each; plague x2 in winter and x1.5 under a wet sky; famine x3 after a dry spell, x2 in winter), lasts 3 to 6 days, reaches one more town now and then (0.6 a day). Sisters in it lose people (`TakeFrom`, never below 3). In this town: plague starts the grey plague outbreak (`CatchPlague`); famine makes everything cost 1.3x (`Cost`), mood -0.08, 0.6 a day someone dies of hunger and otherwise 0.4 a day someone emigrates (never below 4 dot-men). Caravans come only from sisters outside the blight, half as often (0.63; see Food).
- **Struck sisters** (`Struck`): a sister in a blight or war loses gold faster (1.2 or 1.5 a check), a friendly sister (relation 0.25+) gets 8 gold from us (we need 15), and the weakest can collapse. A burn-down in a poor town (gold under 8) also calls `AskAid`, which now skips the foe.
- Not saved: wars and blights in progress (only the dead, the gold and the sister changes persist). Charter ends our war (the old town keeps its soldiers). `ForgetSister` ends a war or blight when its sister is gone.
- Measured (`--soak 600000 --units 1500 --wars`, 8 regions of 183 days, with against without, same seeds): a war about every 31 days, we lose about 1.5 times as often as we win (sisters are bigger and often harsher; mirrors the mod), a blight starts in some town about every 70 days (plague 0.9 and famine 0.6 per 100 days in this town alone, then spreading), hunger kills 0.7 per 100 days, aid is sent 5 times per 100 days, mean dot-men 70 against 68 and gold 45 against 50, so no collapse.
- Test flags: `--war` (a screenshot just after the march), `--blight plague|famine`, `--soak N --units M --wars` (appends the with-and-without table; `Sim.NoStrife` switches wars and blights off).

## Curfew babies and the youth brigade (`Births.cs`, v0.56, 2026-11-12)
- **Curfew babies:** under a rule with curfew (theocracy, junta, party state) weddings come 1.5 times as often (`Population`), and 40% of births get a curfew-baby crier line. Harsh rule has low mood, which slows weddings, so a curfew town ends up a little above a gentle one rather than far above.
- **Youth brigade:** under a rule with patrols (junta, party state) a child deciding what to do in the day (`Decide`, daylight 0.4+) joins the nearest patrolling guard 40% of the time (`JoinBrigade`, at most 2 per guard). `StepBrigade` (after `StepGuards` in `Tick`) keeps them one step behind (`Walker.PX/PY`, the guard's last footprint) or walks them back if left behind; `StepWalker` skips them. At curfew the guard walks to the child's door and the child goes in (`Release` puts the guard back on patrol). A child leaves the brigade if grown, sick, taken, at a festival, or if the guard goes. One crier line a day each for joining and the walk home. `Walker.Follow` is not saved.
- Test flag: `--brigade` (a party state at mid-morning with three newborns; prints how many marched and whether all were home after curfew; with `--shot` it saves the picture).

## Weather and festivals (`Life.cs`, v0.48, 2026-11-11)
- Rain or snow on the square (`Stormy`) puts a festival off: `StartFestival` stores its line and colours (`festWait`) and says so; when the sky clears (within 2 days) it runs, otherwise it lapses. A festival under way is broken up when the weather turns (mood -0.02). A rally (harsh rule 0.8+) is ordered, so it goes on in any weather. All in `StepFestWeather`, called from `StepLife`. Not saved.
- Where (0.80.1): a festival gathers round `festLot` (bunting and dancers). A finished public building (`FinishWorks`) holds its festival round that building's own lot; every other festival (tier-up, harvest, victory, coronation, fairs, matches) stays on the square (lot 0). A put-off festival remembers its lot (`festWaitLot`). Dot-men already gathered drift over as they mingle (each mingle calls `JoinFestival`). Talk says where it is through `FestPlace` ("the square" or "the new <building>"): the Messenger status, a friend's festival line, and `{fest}` in `voices.txt` (filled by `Fill`; 0.80.2). Not saved.
- Measured in the same soak: with the same seeds a region puts off a festival about 5 times per 100 days and breaks one up about 1.4 times.

## Illness (`Disease.cs`, 2026-11-11)
- Five illnesses in `Diseases` (marsh fever, winter cough, sweating sickness, dust lung, grey plague), each weighted by scheme (`Where`) and season (`When`). `MaybeOutbreak` runs from `Events()` every quarter day (`OutbreakOdds` 0.05, scaled by crowding; water-borne marsh fever is rarer and slower with a well or fountain). One outbreak at a time (`Ill`).
- A case hides for `IncubDays` (walking about, half as catching), then shows (`Walker.Sick`: goes home to bed, tinted by the illness). While sick, severity (`SevDay`, slowed by `Care()`) races immunity (`ImmDay` times the dot's own `Resist` 0.6..1.4): immunity first means recovery and `ImmuneDays` of protection, severity first means death. Spread: everyone in the same building (`Room`) as a case may catch it each `DisEvery` ticks (`SpreadK`).
- `Care()`: church or cathedral 0.25, commune or council 0.1, plus leader traits, at most 0.7. Rule 0.7+ harsh or a Cruel leader seals the sick houses (spread x0.4, mood down). The crier announces the first visible case with the town's answer; the end line gives cases and deaths (chronicled). A leader can die of it.
- Not saved: an outbreak in progress ends on reload (the dead stay dead).
- Measured (`--soak 10 --units 150 --ill`, 40 outbreaks each): a grown town with a church loses about 16% of plague cases under a mayor and 7% under a council; a small town with no church about 47%. A junta's quarantine keeps plague to about 8 cases instead of 28. Common illnesses rarely kill when there is care.
- Test flags: `--sick <key>` for a screenshot with an outbreak under way; `--soak N --ill` appends the illness table to `soak.txt`.

## Leader traits (`Gov.cs` `Traits`, `Politics.cs`, 2026-11-11)
- Each leader has two traits from eight (Able, Idle, Cruel, Merciful, Honest, Cunning, Proud, Tolerant); clashing pairs never together. Since 0.68: 32 traits, one per group plus a rare wildcard, and the leader keeps their citizen traits (see Character and bonds). Harsh rule favours Cruel and Proud, gentle rule Merciful and Tolerant (`Traits.Weight`). A monarch's heir keeps one parent trait 60% of the time.
- Effects: mood level, assassination, revolt and coup, arrest (a Cruel leader arrests even outside a Party state), emigration, scandal odds, care for the sick, treasury income, quarantine. A Proud leader always gets a statue. After a day in office the title gains an epithet from the first trait ("Mayor Ada the Honest").
- Saved as `LeaderTraits`; an older save rolls traits for the sitting leader. Shown under the rule in the legend (hover for the blurbs) and on the seat's hover tip. `--soak` appends how often each trait appears per rule.

## Leader health (`Health.cs`, v0.54, 2026-11-12, RimWorld style)
- **Who:** every leader and every former leader still living in town has a `Body` (the owner's choice: leaders only; notables and sister rulers stay names). Kept in `Sim.Bodies` by dot-man id; a body whose dot-man has left or died is dropped. `Retire` gives a leader leaving office (step down, coup, lost election, rule change) the title "former Mayor Ada the Honest"; a former leader appointed again keeps their name and body. Former leaders are never sent to war. A charter clears the bodies (they stay with the old town).
- **Age:** a year per town day (`YearMs`), chosen so age matters within a reign; the seasons' 16-day year is cosmetic. Start age by rule (`StartAge`): elders 58 to 76, a crown's heir 18 to 45, commune 25 to 55, temple 45 to 70, others 35 to 65.
- **Body parts** (`Parts`: size, vital, bleed, what losing it leaves) and **wounds** (`Hurts`: stab, cut, crossbow, blow, burns, broken bone, surgery). A wound has a severity on a part; small vital parts are fragile (heart x2, neck x1.6). It bleeds fully until tended (after 0.02 to 0.12 days, sooner with care 0.3+), then at 3% (times 1 - care); blood refills at 0.6 a day less the bleeding. Tending may let it fester (`Infect` times 1 - tending quality); an infection races immunity (`Toughness`: the dot's luck times `Wear`, which falls with age past 50, frailty, a weak heart and blood loss). Healed wounds of 0.6+ leave a scar or a named after-effect (limp, stiff arm); a destroyed non-vital part is lost (an eye, a hand, a lung).
- **Death** (`BodyDies`): a destroyed vital part, blood at 0, an infection or poison reaching 1, a growth reaching 1, a fatal heart attack or stroke (0.5 - 0.3 x care). The line names the cause, how long after the attempt, and one complaint that helped ("Mayor Ada the Honest, 67, dies of an infected stab wound in the left leg, 2 days after an attempt on their life, weakened by a weak heart."). The sitting leader's death is chronicled and succeeded as before (`Death(lw, line, murdered)`; an assassin's wounds that kill later still bring the crackdown or army takeover, `Murdered`). Former leaders' deaths go to the crier only (`Say(text, note: false)`) so the chronicle does not fill with them.
- **Age complaints** (Gompertz curve `OldK * e^((years - 30) / 10)`, `OldK` 0.011): weak heart (attacks 0.12 a day, each survived makes the next likelier), growth in an organ (found at 0.35, cut out by the physician with care 0.2+, which leaves a surgery wound that can fester), stroke (or a weak side), frailty from 65 (slower healing, more falls), falls, and harmless complaints (bad back, cataracts, poor hearing). Limps, a bad back or a stroke slow the walk by one step period.
- **Assassination is an attempt** (`Attempt`): knife (1 to 3 stabs), crossbow (head-heavy), club (blows, broken bones) or poison (a race like illness); the weapon leans by rule (poison at court, temple and guild; crossbows under 0.8+ harsh rule). Too much blood lost before help comes kills on the spot. Harsh rule still cracks down after a survived attempt.
- **Other links:** illness resistance times `Wear`; a leader or former leader caught in a fire is burned and carried out instead of dying (`BurnBody`); a former leader can die of hunger (named); an ailing elected leader steps down about every 6 days instead of 17. The physician writes about wounds, heart attacks, strokes, growths, surgery and falls; only the sitting leader's health is news.
- **Shown:** the government band shows age and a coloured word (in good health, ailing, wounded, gravely ill) after the traits, with the full chart on hover (`HealthLines`); building hover tips show the leader's and former leaders' health (`HealthAt`). Saved as `Bodies` (with every condition); older saves give the leader a body on the first health step.
- Measured (`--soak 20000 --health`): attempts kill at once 34 to 43%, later 12 to 18%, survived 39 to 55% (a council with a church at the low end). Lifespan from appointment, age alone: at 25 about 34 days, 40 about 22, 50 about 15, 60 about 10, 70 about 6 (the old flat roll gave about 6.4 days at any age; the owner asked for roughly twice as long).
- Test flags: `--hurt` (a screenshot with a wounded 61-year-old leader), `--hurt-tip` (the same with the health chart open), `--soak N --health` (the table above plus reign endings in long runs).
- Fixed on the way: the chronicle's `Major` pattern now matches `assassin...` and `overthr...` words (the trailing word boundary had stopped "is assassinated" from being chronicled).

## Pacing, odds and ids (2026-11-11)
- **Window pacing** (`Program.cs` main loop): 60 fps with the mouse over the window, 30 focused, 15 in the background, 2 minimised (`--shot` runs stay at 60). The town picture (`Frame`, `UploadFrame`) is rebuilt only on frames where a tick ran. `dt` is capped at 1 s so slow pacing never slows the sim. Measured: minimised 0.4% of a core (was 8.6%); hovered about 7.8% (the ImGui panels dominate; lower the 60 if that matters).
- **Odds over time** (`Politics.cs`: `PerDay`, `Odds`, `OncePer`): timed events in `Politics()`, `Region()`, `Events()` and `MaybeOutbreak` are written per town day ("about once every N days"), so changing `PolStepMs` keeps their frequency. The old per-check numbers were multiplied by 4 (checks per day), so small odds are unchanged; large ones (emigration, arrests) are a little lower because they now saturate properly. One-off branches (0.5, 0.4, a coup's chance of a junta) stay plain `Chance()`. The gold and relation drifts in `Region()` are still amounts per check.
- **Dot-man ids** (`Walker.Id`, `Sim.NextId`, `SetId`, `Trait01`): each dot-man has a saved id; pace, night owl, lane and illness resistance (`Trait01(id, 100 + disease)`) come from a hash of it, so they are the same person after a reload and these draws no longer use `Rng`. Older saves get ids on first load.
- **Test folder:** set `DOTBURG_DIR` to save somewhere other than `%APPDATA%\Dotburg` (used to check that ids survive a reload without touching the real town).
Each stage should be checked against the mod (same behaviour, not the same RNG stream) before the next. No emojis; comments in plain language.

## Look and layout (0.53.0, Windows XP / MSN 7 style, `Panels.cs`)
- Text rendering (0.71, `XpText.cs`): every font is drawn by Windows GDI (`GetGlyphOutline`, hinted, as XP drew it) and copied into ImGui's atlas as custom glyphs, instead of ImGui's soft stb_truetype text. The font's `gasp` table picks crisp black-and-white or grey per size, like XP's "Standard" smoothing (at 18 px every face comes out crisp). ImGui.NET's cimgui has no FreeType and there is no C toolchain here, so GDI was chosen over a native FreeType build; it is also the real XP rasteriser. Glyphs use their first pixel column, so `Area` windows have no border (a 1 px border clipped it). Test flags: `--stb` (old smoothed text, to compare), `--font-sheet out.png` (dump the atlas and exit).
- Font (0.53): Tahoma at 18 px with Tahoma Bold (`boldFont`, `BoldText`, `BoldW`, `BoldItem`) for titles and names (`LoadFont`); both ship with Windows. Falls back to Lucida Console (0.50 to 0.52), Segoe UI, Pixelify Sans, then the ImGui default, with faux bold (text drawn twice) when Tahoma Bold is missing.
- Luna blue chrome (0.53, replaced the Win98 set of 0.50 to 0.52): `XpWindow` draws a whole window (blue frame with rounded top corners, glossy title bar Bd + `TitleH` tall with a white bold title and shadow, beige `Face` inside); `Sunken` is a field with XP's thin blue-grey edge; `Chip` a small tile (the banner); `CaptionButton` the glossy blue minimise/restore button; `Blocks` XP progress bars (rounded trough, glossy blocks; green for growth); `Grad` a vertical gradient. Colours at the top of `Panels.cs` (`Face`, `Frame`, `Select`, `XpGreen`, `Desk`). The 4 px round the windows is desk blue (`DeskCol`). The Messenger has MSN 7's pale blue band behind "me" and blue selection; toasts are small Luna windows with a white-to-blue body (no empty title row when a toast has no title). Tooltips stay pale yellow; scrollbars are rounded pale blue. Still no desktop picture: the window is the program (0.51). Chrome and the town picture go on the ImGui background draw list; text sits in see-through ImGui windows (`Area`) laid over it. Real Windows frame kept (snapping, maximise).
- `DrawUi` fills the whole window with one group: spare width goes to the Messenger, spare height to the town window round the picture (the picture keeps a whole-number scale). the town window (title = town and tier; government band with banner, rule, leader, traits, treasury and the "Townsfolk" mood bar; the picture, with crier toasts rising over it since 0.52), the ledger under it (title = day, season, weather; dot-men as one bar split by colour, buildings and the next thing, sister icons and the newest sister, a full-width Troubles row), and the Messenger to the right if there is 320 px for it (0.52; it replaced the Chronicle panel, see below). Labels sit beside the bars, never on them (fixes white on yellow). The ledger rolls up with its title-bar button (not saved).
- Window size (0.51.0): starts hidden, and after the first frame `FitWindow` sizes it to the panels at the largest scale that fits the monitor (`FittedSize`, Messenger at `MsgW` 440), centres it, then shows it. After a resize (0.51.1, `SnapWindow`, 0.3 s after the last change) the window snaps to the fitted size of the nearest scale (between the 560 px minimum height and what fits the monitor), top-left kept; rolling the ledger up or down refits the height (`RefitHeight`). Maximise and F11 are left alone and just stretch the same layout. Windows half-screen snapping gets undone by this. H hides the panels (town at full size on black). Maximised, the window drops the Windows title bar (0.76.2, `FrameOnMaximize`: undecorated while maximised; fitted to the monitor work area by Win32 `FitWorkArea`, since a frameless maximised window would cover the taskbar; the normal place and size are remembered and put back on restore). While maximised, minimise, restore and close buttons sit at the right of the rightmost XP title bar, or the top-right corner with the panels hidden (0.77.1, `WindowButtons`); a double-click on the XP title bars also restores. Restoring goes through Win32 `ShowWindow` (`RestoreFromMax`). The version shows faintly in the town window title bar (`VersionTag`). Test flag: `--size WxH` for a screenshot at a given window size.

## Messenger (0.52, `Messenger.cs`, `MessengerUi.cs`)
- MSN Messenger style, read only (the owner's choice; reply buttons are an idea on the mechanics page). The right panel: "me" (our leader: display picture, `MeName`, `MePresence`, `MeMessage`), then contact groups: this town (the Chronicle bot, notables), Sisters, Group chats, Offline; then the open conversation ("X says:", day breaks, a pale yellow notice when the contact is not Online, a status line under it: "X is typing a message..." or "Last message received on day N").
- **Who talks:** each sister with our leader (`Tell(sister, event)`: her line from `TheySay`, then our reply from `WeSay`, gentle or harsh by `G.Harsh >= 0.6`, shown after 3 to 9 s of "typing", `Due`); two sisters in a group chat (`Gossip`: pacts, quarrels, their wars, absorption); notables of this town (`Npc(role, event)`: captain for war, physician for illness, warden for fires, ringleader under harsh rule 0.5+, steward for food (0.61), named from a hash so they stay put; a charter sends them Offline with the old town). Sisters sign in when founded and off when absorbed or collapsed. Small talk and the ringleader's grumbles come from `Chatter2()` in `Events()`.
- **Hooks:** every chat call sits next to an existing `Say`, which is unchanged (chronicle, soak keys). Talk uses its own `chatRng` and `RelPeek` (never `RelOf`, which fixes a relation), so the Messenger never changes what happens in the town.
- **Status:** sisters Busy at war (ours or hers), Away in a blight, Blocked at relation -0.45 or below, else Online; notables Busy while their trouble is on, else Away; me Busy in war, fire or illness, Away at night. Personal messages per rule (`Mottos`) plus attitude, or the current trouble.
- **Toasts:** `Say` queues a crier toast; a chat line in the same tick replaces it (`Pop`). Up to three rise from the bottom right of the town picture, stay 6 s, sink away; click one to open its chat. Urgent ones (war declared on us, plague, a great fire) also set `NudgeTick`, which shakes the panel for 0.6 s.
- **Emoticons:** codes such as `:)` `(L)` `(bah)` written as separate words are drawn from `resources/emoticons.png` (66: the 40 classics in rows 0 to 3, cut by `resources/make-emoticons.ps1`, and 26 more in rows 4 to 6 from the owner's MSN screenshot `resources/msn-emoticon-sheet.png`, cut by `resources/add-emoticons.ps1`: `(zz)` `(jail)` `(puke)` `(hen)` `(cow)` `(jester)` `(cop)` `(bouquet)` `(clover)` `(horse)` `(peace)` `(Y)` `(!)` `(6)` `(E)` `(crown)` `(hammer)` `(trophy)` `(apple)` `(knife)` `(meal)` `(medal)` `(bear)` `(gran)` `(case)` `(food)`; 10 cells of 24 px across, backgrounds made transparent). `Rich` draws and wraps text with them. Never emoji characters.

## Food and hunger (`Food.cs`, v0.61, 2026-11-12, Songs of Syx style)
- **Supply is not access.** Food exists as goods in places: `Barn` (the town's granary: grain, greens) and `Stock[lot, good]` per building. Goods: Grain, Flour, Bread, Greens, Meals (home pantry). Each citizen has `Food` (0..1, falls `HungerPerDay` 0.9 a day, kids 0.6x, asleep 0.4x); below `EatAt` 0.6 they walk to the nearest counter (shop or market), their pantry, or the mill if the rule opens the granary, and buy a meal. Hungry under 0.45, weak under 0.15 (walks slower), starving at 0 for `StarveDays` 2 and they die (never the leader, never below `MinPop`).
- **Chain:** fields (farmers are a share of the town by tier, sow at spring from `Seed`, crop stands at autumn, reaped over days, winter rots what is left) -> barn -> mill (flour) -> bakery (bread) -> shops and market. Without a mill a bakery grinds by hand (`QuernPerDay`). Greens go barn -> sellers. Carriers are ordinary citizens taking a `Job` (`TakeJob` in `Decide`, 60%, by day, not in storms); a sack pixel shows the good they carry.
- **Buffers and spoilage:** `RotPerDay` per good (bread 0.35 a day, so bakeries bake about 0.9 days ahead); barn grain rots 1% a day, 4% under a wet sky; famine triples it and erodes the standing crop. After harvest, seed is set aside (1.2x what spring needs); a starving town eats its seed corn.
- **Rule matters (`FoodRules`):** days of reserve kept beyond the next harvest, how much families hoard in a panic, whether the granary is open to the public. Gentle rules open it; mayor, guild, crown, junta, party keep it shut, so people can starve beside a full mill. Surplus above the reserve is sold (gold). `Panic` (queues, ration lines, steward warnings) makes families hoard extra pantry meals.
- **Links:** growth waits for food (`FedEnough`, NextThing says "a newcomer, when there is food to spare"); newcomers bring 16 grain; weddings x0.3 under 3 days of food; mood `-= HungerMood()` (replaced the flat famine -0.08); illness immunity x0.7 when hungry; a burn-down loses the store; famine blight now hits the real crop and aid brings grain (+40); caravans bring grain when short; hungry families leave. The steward (a new notable) talks in the Messenger.
- **Shown:** ledger Food row (days of food, bar split by good, note, tooltip: since 0.62 drawn by `FoodTip` in `Panels.cs` from `GetFoodReport`, with days of food in bold, an XP bar with a marker at the grain the rule holds back (days to harvest + reserve), a goods table by barn and town with colour swatches and dashes for none, fields and crop, carriers, hungry and starving counts coloured only when non-zero, and the rule as a grey footnote; test flag `--food-tip`); Troubles line; building hover lists stores; starving dot-men tinted.
- **On the picture (0.64, `DrawFoodSigns` in `Render.cs`, queues in `Food.cs`):** shop and market windows show bread (or porridge grain) on the left and greens on the right, dark when bare; a market's upper windows only when 10+; a bakery's two windows at 1+ and 12+ loaves (daytime, unlit windows only). Sacks by the mill (one per 60 grain, up to 6) and at the west gate for the barn (one per 80, up to 6, a green pixel for greens). A guard stands at each counter door while a rationing rule (hoard 0, with guards) is in a panic. A caravan bringing grain has a gold wagon with sacks on top (`cart.Good`). Hoarders who bought 2+ extra walk home with a bread pixel (`Walker.Bag`, not saved).
- **Queues (0.64):** a counter (shop, market, bakery) serves one dot-man at a time (`servingUntil`, `ServeSecs` 1.2 per meal bought, so hoarders are slow). Arrivals at a busy or bare counter stand in line on the road left of the door (`PlaceInLine`, bending right at the town edge), patience 18 to 35 s, then `Decide` takes them out (`LeaveLine`) and they won't look again for 15 to 30 s; `GoTo` also takes anyone out of a line. Over `MaxQueue` 8 they give up at the sight of it. `StepQueues` runs every tick. A bare counter keeps its line until restocked or patience ends: the bread line. Not saved. Measured (`--soak 160000 --food`): a line of 3+ somewhere 3% of the time, longest 8; starved 26 against 65 in 0.63 (people now wait for a restock instead of walking off), hungry share 5.2%.
- **Saved:** `Food` and `Starve` per dot, stores, barn, crop, standing, seed, panic, season. Older saves get a fresh start (`FreshFood`); carried loads are put back at their destination.
- Measured (`--soak 160000 --food`, 6 regions, about 49 days each): 18 starved, 9 families left hungry, mean hungry share 7%. Tuning numbers sit at the top of `Food.cs`.
- Test flags: `--hungry` (screenshot of a hungry town), `--soak N --food` (timeline every 2 days at noon per region).
- **Panic and imports (0.63, from a study of food-crisis literature):** panic follows what dot-men see, not the true stores. `PanicSignal` = half `ShelfFill` (counter stock against a normal 0.4 of room, or pantries in a hamlet with no counter) + half `FoodDays / 6`, less `Rumour` and 0.1 if someone was just turned away. The target is an S-curve, `1 / (1 + e^(-10 (0.35 - signal) - 3 Panic))`; the `3 Panic` term makes panic hold itself up, so it rises at 3 a day and eases at 0.5 a day and outlasts the shortage by a few days. `Rumour` (`Hear`, fades 0.15 a day, not saved): failed harvest +0.25, too little seed +0.15, famine here +0.3, famine in a sister +0.1, a food store burnt up to +0.2. Caravans: only from sisters not struck (`Struck(s) == 0`), half as often while any blight runs (they used to stop entirely). `RegionStress` = share of towns (sisters and us) in a blight or war. A caravan's grain (`CaravanFood`, only under 4 days of food): a harsh (0.5+) or guild sister withholds it when stress is over 0.3 (crier line); otherwise grain comes with odds `1 - 0.8 stress`, `1 - 0.5 stress` of the old amount, at `2 (1 + 2 stress)` gold, never if we can't pay.
- Measured (`--soak 160000 --food`, same 6 regions): starved 65 against 71 in 0.62, mean hungry share 5.5% against 5.3%, mean panic 0.19, over 0.5 for 15% of samples (late summer before the harvest, lingering a few days after). With the shelf fill measured against full room instead, panic sat over 0.5 64% of the time and deaths rose to 100: `ShelfNormal` matters. The soak timeline shows `panic` and `shelf`; the summary shows mean panic and grain withheld.
- Ideas left (on the mechanics page): bread riots, purses and prices, granary fires, drawn fields, and from the 0.63 study: coalition-first feeding and coercion that fails when guards go unfed (selectorate), counter queues with a service rate, extraction that grows with tenure, war ratcheting harshness.

## Settlers (0.73, `FormRule` in `Politics.cs`)
- A new town starts with no rule (`GovKey == "none"`, "Settlers", base mood 0.6, harshness 0) and no leader; `Politics()` does nothing else until there are `RuleAt` (5) grown dot-men. Then they settle on a hamlet rule, weighted 1 each plus: elders 1.5 per adult of 50+, headman 1.2 per ambitious, brave, proud or hot-headed adult, shrine keepers 1.5 per pious or doomsayer, founding family 1.2 per friendship among them. The line names the reason and is chronicled ("settles on" is in `Major`). `IsHamlet("none")` is true, so every hamlet check (no plots, no coups, the Village choice) applies; `MaybeChooseGov` waits until a rule is formed. A rule is never dissolved if the town later drops below 5. Older saves keep their rule. Measured (`--soak 30000`): the rule formed on day 2.

## Citizen health (`Lives.cs`, 0.67)
- **Ages:** everyone ages a year every 4 town days (`YearMs` in `Health.cs`, was one a day); the leaders' age-driven rates (complaint onsets, heart attacks, growth swelling, falls) and the sister rulers' age and fall rates are scaled by `AgeRate` (0.25) so they are per year. Saves before `V = 2` stretch leader and sister-ruler birth times by 4 on load so everyone keeps their age. Leaders now live about 4 times as many town days (the owner's choice: citizens and leaders on one clock).
- **Citizens:** `Walker.Born` (saved as age in years, `Dot.Y`); settlers, newcomers and older-save dot-men get 18 to 58 from their id; a newborn is 0 and turns 16 on growing up (`GrowUp`). Names: see Names (0.78). The old (70+) walk one step slower.
- **Death, sister-ruler style:** checked once every `LivesEvery` food steps per dot-man (offset by id). Age rate `CitOldK` 0.004 per year at 30, e-fold every 10 years, less up to half for care, shared between in their sleep (mostly 60+), a weak heart, a stroke (mostly 50+), a growth, a fever or winter chill. Accidents per day: a fall from a hayrick (autumn, 1/1500), under a cart's wheels (carriers, 1/2500), a slip on the ice (snow, 55+, 1/400), through the pond ice (winter, park, under 40, 1/3000), a fall from the scaffolding (works/6000), kicked by a horse (a cart in town, 1/4000), the cold (winter, starving or 75+, 1/200). Never leaders or former leaders (they have bodies), never children, never below `MinPop`. Ordinary causes set `Walker.Fading`: they walk home (`Decide`) and die there; sudden ones die where they are (`PassAway`).
- **Told once every 4 days:** `Mourn` collects every death (hunger, illness and fire call it too) and at daybreak every 4th day `BurialSummary` says one crier line ("2 buried in X these 4 days: Edda, 57, of a weak heart; and Sim, 37, through the ice on the pond.") and the physician writes; with no deaths the physician sometimes cheers instead. Not in the chronicle.
- Measured (`--soak 300000 --units 150`, 92 days): 19 summaries, about 1.6 deaths per 4 days, the town reached City with 58 dot-men.

## Character and bonds (`Bonds.cs`, 0.68)
- **Traits for everyone:** 32 traits in `Traits.All` (Gov.cs), in groups (`Cat`): heart (cruel, merciful, proud, tolerant, humble, jealous, loyal, spiteful, generous), work (able, idle, honest, cunning, greedy, thrifty, reckless, careful, ambitious), spirit (brave, craven, pious, cynical, cheerful, gloomy, hot-headed, calm, curious), and five rare wildcards (firebug, vengeful, doomsayer, light-fingered, charmer; `WildChance` 0.06). Every dot-man has one of each group and maybe a wildcard (`CitizenTraits`, from a hash of the id, cached, never saved; a clashing pick moves on in its group). Leaders and sister rulers have the same shape (`RollTraits`). The leader effect columns (mood, kill, revolt, arrest, flee, scandal, care, gold) apply to leaders as before; with three or four traits the products are a little wider.
- **Leaders come from the townsfolk:** `Appoint` weighs each candidate by their own traits under the rule (`LeaderFit`: `Traits.Weight` per trait, ambitious x2.5, charmer x3, an heir sharing a parent trait x3, a dotlet x0.05) and keeps their citizen first name and traits. The epithet is the heart trait. Names: see Names (0.78).
- **Bonds:** `Walker.Bonds` (at most 3: `Bond` with Who, Kind grudge or friend, Str 0..1, Why, Day; saved in `Dot.B`). About once a day an awake dot-man indoors with company does something to a roommate (`Meet`, `MeetsPerDay` 2.5 of awake indoor time): an old grudge flares (hot-headed: fists) or the calm and humble cool it; the cruel bully (dotlets harder), the light-fingered pick pockets, the hot-headed fly at people, the jealous envy, two proud quarrel, the cunning and greedy cheat at dice; otherwise friendship, likelier for the warm traits, near certain for a charmer, never with the cruel. Grudges grow faster for the vengeful (x2) and spiteful (x1.5), slower for the calm and cheerful; they fade slowly (deep ones hardly; never for the vengeful); friendships fade in days (loyal slower). A grudge reaching 0.6 is said once ("X will never forgive Y: Y spat at X at the bakery, on day 33.").
- **Motives:** friends of anyone arrested hate the leader (`TakenNews`, loyal more); a cruel leader arrests those who hate them first. The deepest grudge against the leader (`MotiveAgainstLeader`) adds `MotiveK` 0.1 x strength a day to the assassination odds, even in a hamlet. A grudge of 0.3+ names the assassin (`Attempt(lw, by)`), the line tells where it began, then the assassin is seized (70%) or flees to a sister; under 0.8+ harsh rule their friends are rounded up first.
- **Other hooks:** firebugs start a fire about once in 20 days (`Wildcard`; seen running 40%), doomsayers feed `Rumour` and cry doom (at most every 1.5 days); greedy hoard x1.5, thrifty and generous x0.5 (Food.cs); reckless accidents x2, careful x0.5 (Lives.cs); the craven never fight fires, the brave come from further (Fires.cs). Hover tips list up to 4 people in a building with traits and their strongest feeling (`PeopleAt`).
- Measured (`--soak 300000 --units 150`, 92 days, ~95 dot-men): about 2,800 meetings, 2,000 bonds made; at the end about 70 grudges (a few deep) and 130 friendships; quarrel lines at most one a day; named assassinations happen under harsh rule where arrests feed grudges. The soak's `--- bonds ---` section shows the counts and the story lines.

## Names (`Names.cs`, 0.78)
- **Saved, not hashed.** `Walker.First` and `Walker.Last` (saved as `Dot.Fn`, `Dot.Ln`) are given once by `Christen` when a dot-man is made (`NewWalker`) or born. Before 0.78 names were a hash of the id from 40 first and 32 family names, so a town of 60 had many namesakes; a hash cannot promise uniqueness, a saved name can.
- **Unique in town:** `Christen` tries up to 80 seeded draws (seed from `Trait01(id, 900 + k)`, never `Rng`) and takes the first whose first name nobody living has; after 50 tries a shared first name is allowed, a shared full name never. Measured: every town in an 18-region soak had all names distinct.
- **Five tongues:** English, Old English, Norse, Celtic (Welsh, Irish, Scots, Cornish), Low Countries. Every town leans toward one (`Names.Lean` from the town name and founding number: a base mix plus 0.55 for its home tongue), so towns sound different while newcomers keep the mix varied. Family names follow the given name's tongue 82% of the time, else drift English.
- **How names are built:** given names from curated lists (481 listed), Old English two-part names (Ed + ric, Wulf + stan, Hild + gyth: 30 fronts, 32 backs), pet forms (Hobkin, Tibby), word names (Bramble, Mercy) and Dotburg's own homegrown names (the old 40 and more, 8% everywhere). Family names from trades, place compounds (Ash + ford, Lind + berg, Tre + wen), patronymics (Gilbertson, Gunnarsen), Mac names, van der names, nicknames (Lightfoot) and features (Underhill). Generated names are checked: no word twice (Wulf + ulf), no letter doubled at the join, no run of 5 consonants, no rude substring, no first name inside the family name. 2000 draws for one town: about 930 distinct first names, 890 family names, 1990 full names.
- **Families:** a child takes a parent's family name (a married couple's when there is one); 15% of newcomers share a family name with someone already in town (kin; at least 4 families needed).
- **Others:** notables (`NpcTitle`) take a given name from the town's lean, seeded by role, town and founding; sister rulers from their own town's lean (`RulerOf`, `RulerName`, one `Rng` draw as before). Leaders keep their citizen first name, as since 0.68.
- **Older saves:** everyone keeps their old hashed name (`LegacyName`), since bonds, the chronicle and the leader use it; anyone sharing a first or full name with someone earlier is named anew (`RenameDuplicates`), except the leader and former leaders. Notables' names changed once in 0.78.
- Test flag: `--names` writes `names.txt`: 12 sample people for six towns, the repeat counts, and, with a save in `DOTBURG_DIR`, that save's townsfolk (loads, never saves).

## Weddings between friends (`Births.cs`, 0.78)
- **Who marries:** `Wedding` (odds as before: 0.12 a day x (0.5 + mood), x1.5 curfew, x0.3 under 3 days of food) chooses among the free (`FreeToWed`: grown citizens, well, not leading, not leaving, unwed or widowed). Under a strict rule (harshness 0.5+: temple, crown, junta, party) a share of `0.6 x harshness` of weddings are arranged between any two free (a pair with a grudge: "Neither looks happy."). Otherwise friends marry: pairs with a friendship of `WedFriendsAt` 0.25+ either way and no grudge, weighted by its strength. With no such friends a neighbour makes a match `MatchShare` 40% of the time, else no wedding that roll.
- **After:** `Walker.Spouse` (saved as `Dot.P`) both ways; a friendship of +0.6 (+0.3 arranged) that never fades below `WedBond` 0.4 while both live and is never pushed out by a new bond; the couple share a home if either has room (`MoveIn`, the emptier). A dead spouse leaves the other free to marry again (`SpouseOf` finds only the living). Hover tips say "married to X".
- **Births:** a baby goes to a married couple when there is one (else any parent as before), takes a parent's colour and family name, and is born in their home; the crier names the parents and the dotlet 70% of the time.
- **The old wedding quietly fizzled:** it needed two dot-men standing outdoors at the moment of the roll. Without that filter weddings match their odds: in 18 regions over 123 days, 232 births against 161 in 0.77 (+44%).
- Soak: the `--- bonds ---` section counts weddings by kind and married couples; the food soak prints births, weddings and pop per region.

## Sharing food (`Food.cs`, 0.78)
- **A friend's table:** a dot-man under `HungryAt` who finds no counter, pantry or open granary looks for the nearest spouse or friend whose friendship toward them is `ShareFriendAt` 0.15+, whose home is not burning and holds a meal to spare (a spouse or a generous friend gives their last; others from 2 up; the greedy from 3 up), and walks there to eat (`FriendsTable`, `Walker.FedBy`, not saved). Eating there strengthens both bonds (+0.1 guest, +0.05 host); the crier tells of it at most once a day.
- **Street tables:** in hard times (2+ hungry and a famine, panic over 0.3 or under 2 days of food), `StreetTablesPerDay` 1.5 a day, a generous dot-man with 2+ meals at home feeds up to `StreetMeals` 5 of the hungriest from other homes, keeping one meal. Each fed gains a friendship of 0.15 toward them; mood +0.004 per meal. Under a rule with guards and harshness 0.8+ (junta, party), 35% of tables are broken up as unlawful gatherings and the host takes a grudge of 0.2 against the leader.
- Measured (`--soak 400000 --food`, 18 regions, 123 days each, against 0.77 with the same seeds): starved 1172 against 1438, mean hungry share 11.9% against 13.7%, panic over 0.5 36% of the time against 41%, despite 44% more births. Per region about 2 to 20 meals at a friend's table and 10 to 40 street tables. Six regions were too few to see it: the first 6-region run looked worse (376 against 151) because each region's history diverges.

## The chronicler (`Chronicler.cs`, 0.69)
- A dot-man keeps the chronicle (`ChroniclerId`, `ChroniclerName`, saved). `EnsureChronicler` picks one (adults, not the leader; the curious, honest, pious, cynical and older likelier) at the first note or step and writes an opening line in their voice; when they die, leave or are raised to lead, the next one opens with "X writes no more." or "X lays down the pen to lead".
- Voice = spirit trait (or doomsayer): `Remarks[voice][kind]` adds a remark after about half the lines (`KindOf`: death, power, disaster, triumph, other). Slant: a chronicler with a friendship 0.3+ with the leader praises lines naming the leader (`Praise`) and leaves out 60% of arrest and crackdown lines; a grudge 0.3+ sneers (`Snipes`); someone they love or hate named in a line gets a personal remark. `Interests[voice]` lets a non-major crier line into the chronicle (burials for the gloomy, weddings for the cheerful, strangers for the curious), at most one a half day (`MaybeChronicle`, from `Say`).
- File format: each line ends ` | ~Name (Trait)`; the Messenger heads lines "Name writes (...)" and draws them in `ChatStyles["t:" + voice]` (spirit traits got styles in 0.69). Older lines have no signature and keep the plain look. All choices use `chatRng`.

## Leader's own contacts (`Pals.cs`, 0.69)
- The Messenger is the leader's account. Public (kept on a change): Chronicle, notables, sisters, group chats. Personal (`Convo.Kind == "pal"`, key `pal:<leader id>:<other id>`, `Role` friend or foe): friendships with the leader either way at 0.25+ (stay until 0.12; at most 5) under Friends, grudges either way at 0.35+ (stay until 0.2; at most 4) under Blocked, and the leader before them while alive in town (`PrevLeader`). `StepPals` (every 27 ticks) keeps the list; contacts who die, leave or are taken go Offline.
- `NewLeader`: on a change the old personal chats are deleted, `PalLeader` moves on, and how the old leader left (`FallHow`, set in `Coup`, `Election`, the commune turn and `StepDown`) becomes a real bond: a coup a 0.6 grudge, a lost vote a grudge or a handshake, a hand-over a friendship. This is the one part that changes the town (it uses `Rng`); a coup's grudge can feed an assassination.
- Talk: a new contact says hello (congratulations to a new leader, the bond's origin turned to "you"/"me" by `WhyAs`, the old leader's good luck or bitterness, an enemy's last word before the block); friends write about 0.6 times a day (`PalChatter` from `Chatter2`), their own sickness or hunger first, then the leader's, then the town's trouble, else small talk by voice (`PalSmall`); the leader answers by their own voice (`PalReply`). Fonts by `TraitStyle` (the first styled trait), pictures from `PicHash(id * 31 + 7)`, the same as if they led. Half of all leaders show a personal message by their voice (`TraitMottos`).
- Measured (`--soak 300000`, 92 days): 7 to 12 leaders, 5 to 9 friends and 4 to 5 blocked contacts added per run; friendships fade in days, so the list changes. Test flag: `--pals` (two friends, an enemy, and the chronicle in two hands; add `--open bot` for the chronicle).

## Street lamps (`Layout.cs` `PaintStatic`, `Render.cs` `LampOn`, 0.70)
- The mod lit every crossing; the app does not (the owner's choice: too bright, hard to focus). Lamps grow with the tier: none in a hamlet; a village lights the four corners of the seat (hall, else tower, else the middle lot) and the inn; a town adds about 40% of the middle crossings and about 30% of those by a market, church or school; a city lights most of the middle, about half of those by any public building and a quarter of the rest, plus mid-block lamps in front of public buildings. Which crossings is a hash, so they stay put. Lamps on empty ground are never lit. Look unchanged: same pixel and glow.
- From dusk all are on; most go out around midnight (`LampOn`, each a little apart); those by an all-night building (inn, hotel, hall, tower, cathedral) or the seat burn till dawn (`LampLate`). `LampNo` maps lamp and glow pixels to their lamp; the lamp states are part of `baseKey`.
- Test flag: `--phase X` sets the time of day for a screenshot (0 midnight, 0.5 noon; 0.9 is evening, 0.05 late night).
