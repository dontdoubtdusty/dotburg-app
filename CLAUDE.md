# Dotburg app: notes for Claude

A standalone C# / .NET 10 / Raylib-cs + ImGui.NET port of the `/town` Claude Code mod in `../dotburg` (same stack as birb-box and srd-desk). The mod is **frozen at v0.6.0** (the user's choice) and is the spec: when in doubt, read `../dotburg/hooks/register.tsx` and `../dotburg/CLAUDE.md`. The app is powered by its own clock only (one growth unit per 20 s while open, `OwnClock` in `Program.cs`, behind the `IPowerSource` interface so another source could be added); no Claude link.

## Version
- `Version` in `Program.cs` (`"Dotburg 0.52.0"`) is the one version number: window title and `crash.txt`. Stages 1 to 4 were 0.1 to 0.4.
- Versions follow `claude/versioning.md` (`major.minor.patch`): a finished feature bumps minor, a small tweak bumps patch, in the same change (not at wrap up). Bigger jumps only when the owner says. The Win98 UI pass made it `0.50.0` (0.49 had been bumped in code only). Note the new number in the change summary and in the session log row.
- After raising it, run `Build Dotburg.bat` so the published copy matches.

## The claude/ folder
- At session start, read `claude/file-map.md`, `claude/session-log.md` and `claude/vision.md` before searching the code, then `claude/next-session-plan.md` if the task is the next step. The code wins if they disagree; flag the mismatch.
- When the owner writes "Instructions:", read `claude/instructions-for-claude.md` first.
- At wrap up: update `claude/file-map.md` if files or their jobs changed (and stale line counts); add a dated row to `claude/session-log.md` with the version and add 1 to the session count; update `vision.md` tags for anything built; cut finished steps from `next-session-plan.md`. Also add a row to the root `../claude/session-log.md` (the GitHub-wide log) tagged dotburg-app.
- Before writing a helper, check the file map's "Where to find things" and grep; say "reused X" or "no existing equivalent, added Y" in the change summary.
- `vision.md` and the session log record why, not only what: the choice made, what was rejected, what would make us revisit it.

## Run and test
- Normal use: the Dotburg shortcut (Desktop and Start menu), made by `Build Dotburg.bat`, which publishes to `..\Dotburg Town` (not `Dotburg`: that would clash with the `dotburg` mod folder, since Windows ignores case). Run the bat again after every meaningful change (close the app first). For development: `dotnet build -c Release`.
- `--shot out.png [--units N] [--ticks N]`: render a few frames after pre-growing the town and save a screenshot, then exit (never touches the save). `--soak N`: headless run of N ticks, writes `soak.txt` next to the exe. `--fresh`: ignore the save.
- Files: `%APPDATA%\Dotburg\town.json` (save, every 30 s and on exit; on start a capped catch-up of up to 2 h at half rate) and `chronicle.txt` (major events, appended, plain text).

## Layout (partial class `Sim`)
`Content.cs` data (sprites, palettes, schemes, seasons) / `Sim.cs` state, helpers, day-night, seasons, crier, chronicle queue, `Tick` / `Layout.cs` roads, lots, `PaintStatic` / `Walkers.cs` dot-men / `Growth.cs` units to buildings, upgrades, tiers, scaffolding / `Render.cs` `Frame()` into `Out` (0xRRGGBB, 72x60 px, exactly 7x7 lots) / `Storage.cs` save, chronicle file / `Program.cs` window, texture upload (integer scale), legend and chronicle panels.
- Faithful port first: names and numbers follow the TS. `Round` mimics JS `Math.round`; `Hash` mimics the TS `hash`. The RNG is a seeded `Random` (`Sim.Rng`) so runs can be replayed.

## Fires (`Fires.cs`, v0.46, RimWorld style)
- A `Fire` sits on one lot with a `Size` (0..1, how fierce) and `Burn` (0..1, how much of the building is gone). Once a second (`StepFires`): size grows logistically on fuel (`0.2 * flam * s * (1 - s)`, `Fuel` table: flammability and how long the building lasts); it is cut by rain (`rainAmt`), snow cover, a well or fountain next door (bucket chain, 0.015), a fire brigade from Town tier (0.02) and each dot-man at the blaze (0.01). Burn grows with size; at 1 the building is a ruin. A fierce fire (size over 0.4) can jump to a lot one block away: chance `0.012 * size * neighbour flammability * dryness * (1 - rain)`. Stone buildings (church, cathedral, tower) resist; well and fountain never burn; a park burns out but stays.
- Firefighting: `SendFighters` sends up to 6 (`Headcount / 6`, at least 2) of the nearest free dot-men to the door (`Goal.Fight`, a blue bucket pixel); at night 40% of sleepers wake to the alarm. Sleepers and the bedridden inside wake at 22% a step; if still inside when size passes 0.5, 3% a step die (rare). People never enter a burning lot (`Burning`).
- Ignition: `FireDice` (kitchen or oven fire, about once per 50 days in a 20-building town, more in summer, dry spells (`dryDays`) and for bakeries, inns and mills, never in rain); `Strike` (a lightning flash may hit a building, taller ones likelier; rain nearly always puts it out within seconds); `Arson` (a saboteur, bigger start). At most 6 fires and ruins up to 30% of the lots (`RuinCap`).
- Aftermath (`BurnDown`): the lot becomes `ruin` and a `Ruin` record (what it was, `At`) is kept; gold -4, mood down, ruins and fires weigh on mood. Harsh rule (0.8+) blames a saboteur and arrests (40%); commune or council collect (50%). `RebuildRuins` rebuilds through scaffolding after 1 to 2 days (guild half, poor town 1.6x, rich 0.7x). Ruins are saved (`Ruins` in the save; an older save's ruin without a record rebuilds as a house after a day). Fires in progress are not saved.
- Measured (`--soak 10 --units 150 --fires`, 60 fires each, clear weather): a small town (50 units) loses the building 78% of the time, a mid one (90) 53%, a grown one with a brigade (150) 5%; in rain none, out in about 2 s. Lives lost: none in 60 daytime fires. Soak output lists `Fire!`, `burns down`, spread and rain lines.
- Test flags: `--fire` (screenshot with a fire going), `--soak N --units M --fires` (the table above).

## War and blight (`War.cs`, v0.47, 2026-11-11)
- **War with a sister** (`WarOn`, `MaybeWar`, `StartWar`, `StepWar`): rolled with the events, rate per day `4 * (0.002 + 0.015 * harshness + 0.004 * harsh-sisters)`, at least 5 days after the last war, town over 10. A foe is picked by how harsh it is (and how unlike us). Up to 6 soldiers (12% of the town, never below 4 left, never the leader, children or the sick) walk out of the west gate (`Goal.Leave`, `To = "#war"`, so no sister counts them). The war lasts 1 to 3 days: news from the front, a soldier lost (40% of news), and a raid (`Calm` and 0.35 a day) that calls `Arson`. At the end: odds `0.5 + 0.2 * (our harshness - theirs) + 0.005 * (our headcount - theirs)`, clamped 0.15 to 0.85. Victory: the foe loses a fifth, plunder +5 gold, mood up, a festival, the foe may change rule (40%). Defeat: growth pool lost, -6 gold, mood -0.15, aid asked, then 30% revolution under harsh rule or 20% army takeover under gentle rule. Survivors walk back in (`ReturnSoldiers`, `SettleColour`).
- **While at war:** -2 gold per check, mood -0.06 unless the rule is 0.8+ harsh, no envoys, fairs or caravans, no aid from the foe. Soldiers already past the gate are saved as dot-men at home (`AwayColours`); the war itself is not saved.
- **War between two sisters** (`SisterWarOn`): harsh (0.5+) sisters, rate 0.024 a day each; 1 to 3 days; the loser loses a fifth, then absorbed (10%, if smaller and a fate is ready) or changes rule (40%), and half the time her people reach this gate as refugees (`Refugees`, shared with `SisterRevolt`).
- **Blight** (`BlightOn`, `StepBlight`): one plague or famine at a time, starts in a random town of the region when `Calm` (rate 0.025 a day each; plague x2 in winter and x1.5 under a wet sky; famine x3 after a dry spell, x2 in winter), lasts 3 to 6 days, reaches one more town now and then (0.6 a day). Sisters in it lose people (`TakeFrom`, never below 3). In this town: plague starts the grey plague outbreak (`CatchPlague`); famine makes everything cost 1.3x (`Cost`), mood -0.08, 0.6 a day someone dies of hunger and otherwise 0.4 a day someone emigrates (never below 4 dot-men). No caravans during a blight.
- **Struck sisters** (`Struck`): a sister in a blight or war loses gold faster (1.2 or 1.5 a check), a friendly sister (relation 0.25+) gets 8 gold from us (we need 15), and the weakest can collapse. A burn-down in a poor town (gold under 8) also calls `AskAid`, which now skips the foe.
- Not saved: wars and blights in progress (only the dead, the gold and the sister changes persist). Charter ends our war (the old town keeps its soldiers). `ForgetSister` ends a war or blight when its sister is gone.
- Measured (`--soak 600000 --units 1500 --wars`, 8 regions of 183 days, with against without, same seeds): a war about every 31 days, we lose about 1.5 times as often as we win (sisters are bigger and often harsher; mirrors the mod), a blight starts in some town about every 70 days (plague 0.9 and famine 0.6 per 100 days in this town alone, then spreading), hunger kills 0.7 per 100 days, aid is sent 5 times per 100 days, mean dot-men 70 against 68 and gold 45 against 50, so no collapse.
- Test flags: `--war` (a screenshot just after the march), `--blight plague|famine`, `--soak N --units M --wars` (appends the with-and-without table; `Sim.NoStrife` switches wars and blights off).

## Weather and festivals (`Life.cs`, v0.48, 2026-11-11)
- Rain or snow on the square (`Stormy`) puts a festival off: `StartFestival` stores its line and colours (`festWait`) and says so; when the sky clears (within 2 days) it runs, otherwise it lapses. A festival under way is broken up when the weather turns (mood -0.02). A rally (harsh rule 0.8+) is ordered, so it goes on in any weather. All in `StepFestWeather`, called from `StepLife`. Not saved.
- Measured in the same soak: with the same seeds a region puts off a festival about 5 times per 100 days and breaks one up about 1.4 times.

## Illness (`Disease.cs`, 2026-11-11)
- Five illnesses in `Diseases` (marsh fever, winter cough, sweating sickness, dust lung, grey plague), each weighted by scheme (`Where`) and season (`When`). `MaybeOutbreak` runs from `Events()` every quarter day (`OutbreakOdds` 0.05, scaled by crowding; water-borne marsh fever is rarer and slower with a well or fountain). One outbreak at a time (`Ill`).
- A case hides for `IncubDays` (walking about, half as catching), then shows (`Walker.Sick`: goes home to bed, tinted by the illness). While sick, severity (`SevDay`, slowed by `Care()`) races immunity (`ImmDay` times the dot's own `Resist` 0.6..1.4): immunity first means recovery and `ImmuneDays` of protection, severity first means death. Spread: everyone in the same building (`Room`) as a case may catch it each `DisEvery` ticks (`SpreadK`).
- `Care()`: church or cathedral 0.25, commune or council 0.1, plus leader traits, at most 0.7. Rule 0.7+ harsh or a Cruel leader seals the sick houses (spread x0.4, mood down). The crier announces the first visible case with the town's answer; the end line gives cases and deaths (chronicled). A leader can die of it.
- Not saved: an outbreak in progress ends on reload (the dead stay dead).
- Measured (`--soak 10 --units 150 --ill`, 40 outbreaks each): a grown town with a church loses about 16% of plague cases under a mayor and 7% under a council; a small town with no church about 47%. A junta's quarantine keeps plague to about 8 cases instead of 28. Common illnesses rarely kill when there is care.
- Test flags: `--sick <key>` for a screenshot with an outbreak under way; `--soak N --ill` appends the illness table to `soak.txt`.

## Leader traits (`Gov.cs` `Traits`, `Politics.cs`, 2026-11-11)
- Each leader has two traits from eight (Able, Idle, Cruel, Merciful, Honest, Cunning, Proud, Tolerant); clashing pairs never together. Harsh rule favours Cruel and Proud, gentle rule Merciful and Tolerant (`Traits.Weight`). A monarch's heir keeps one parent trait 60% of the time.
- Effects: mood level, assassination, revolt and coup, arrest (a Cruel leader arrests even outside a Party state), emigration, scandal odds, care for the sick, treasury income, quarantine. A Proud leader always gets a statue. After a day in office the title gains an epithet from the first trait ("Mayor Ada the Honest").
- Saved as `LeaderTraits`; an older save rolls traits for the sitting leader. Shown under the rule in the legend (hover for the blurbs) and on the seat's hover tip. `--soak` appends how often each trait appears per rule.

## Pacing, odds and ids (2026-11-11)
- **Window pacing** (`Program.cs` main loop): 60 fps with the mouse over the window, 30 focused, 15 in the background, 2 minimised (`--shot` runs stay at 60). The town picture (`Frame`, `UploadFrame`) is rebuilt only on frames where a tick ran. `dt` is capped at 1 s so slow pacing never slows the sim. Measured: minimised 0.4% of a core (was 8.6%); hovered about 7.8% (the ImGui panels dominate; lower the 60 if that matters).
- **Odds over time** (`Politics.cs`: `PerDay`, `Odds`, `OncePer`): timed events in `Politics()`, `Region()`, `Events()` and `MaybeOutbreak` are written per town day ("about once every N days"), so changing `PolStepMs` keeps their frequency. The old per-check numbers were multiplied by 4 (checks per day), so small odds are unchanged; large ones (emigration, arrests) are a little lower because they now saturate properly. One-off branches (0.5, 0.4, a coup's chance of a junta) stay plain `Chance()`. The gold and relation drifts in `Region()` are still amounts per check.
- **Dot-man ids** (`Walker.Id`, `Sim.NextId`, `SetId`, `Trait01`): each dot-man has a saved id; pace, night owl, lane and illness resistance (`Trait01(id, 100 + disease)`) come from a hash of it, so they are the same person after a reload and these draws no longer use `Rng`. Older saves get ids on first load.
- **Test folder:** set `DOTBURG_DIR` to save somewhere other than `%APPDATA%\Dotburg` (used to check that ids survive a reload without touching the real town).
Each stage should be checked against the mod (same behaviour, not the same RNG stream) before the next. No emojis; comments in plain language.

## Look and layout (0.50.0, Windows 98 style, `Panels.cs`)
- Font: Lucida Console (`lucon.ttf`, the font of the PowerShell console the mods run in) at 18 px (`LoadFont`); falls back to Segoe UI, Pixelify Sans, then the ImGui default. Segoe UI (0.45) and the pixel font were both turned down.
- Classic Win98 chrome, no desktop behind it (0.51.0: the teal was dropped; the window face is silver): silver faces, two-pixel bevels (`Raised`, `Sunken`), navy-to-blue title bars (`TitleBar`, faux bold), block progress bars (`Blocks`), pale yellow tooltips. Chrome and the town picture go on the ImGui background draw list; text sits in see-through ImGui windows (`Area`) laid over it. Real Windows frame kept (snapping, maximise).
- `DrawUi` fills the whole window with one group: spare width goes to the Messenger, spare height to the town window round the picture (the picture keeps a whole-number scale). the town window (title = town and tier; government band with banner, rule, leader, traits, treasury and the "Townsfolk" mood bar; the picture, with crier toasts rising over it since 0.52), the ledger under it (title = day, season, weather; dot-men as one bar split by colour, buildings and the next thing, sister icons and the newest sister, a full-width Troubles row), and the Messenger to the right if there is 320 px for it (0.52; it replaced the Chronicle panel, see below). Labels sit beside the bars, never on them (fixes white on yellow). The ledger rolls up with its title-bar button (not saved).
- Window size (0.51.0): starts hidden, and after the first frame `FitWindow` sizes it to the panels at the largest scale that fits the monitor (`FittedSize`, Messenger at `MsgW` 440), centres it, then shows it. After a resize (0.51.1, `SnapWindow`, 0.3 s after the last change) the window snaps to the fitted size of the nearest scale (between the 560 px minimum height and what fits the monitor), top-left kept; rolling the ledger up or down refits the height (`RefitHeight`). Maximise and F11 are left alone and just stretch the same layout. Windows half-screen snapping gets undone by this. H hides the panels (town at full size on black). Test flag: `--size WxH` for a screenshot at a given window size.

## Messenger (0.52, `Messenger.cs`, `MessengerUi.cs`)
- MSN Messenger style, read only (the owner's choice; reply buttons are an idea on the mechanics page). The right panel: "me" (our leader: display picture, `MeName`, `MePresence`, `MeMessage`), then contact groups: this town (the Chronicle bot, notables), Sisters, Group chats, Offline; then the open conversation ("X says:", day breaks, a pale yellow notice when the contact is not Online, a status line under it: "X is typing a message..." or "Last message received on day N").
- **Who talks:** each sister with our leader (`Tell(sister, event)`: her line from `TheySay`, then our reply from `WeSay`, gentle or harsh by `G.Harsh >= 0.6`, shown after 3 to 9 s of "typing", `Due`); two sisters in a group chat (`Gossip`: pacts, quarrels, their wars, absorption); notables of this town (`Npc(role, event)`: captain for war, physician for illness, warden for fires, ringleader under harsh rule 0.5+, named from a hash so they stay put; a charter sends them Offline with the old town). Sisters sign in when founded and off when absorbed or collapsed. Small talk and the ringleader's grumbles come from `Chatter2()` in `Events()`.
- **Hooks:** every chat call sits next to an existing `Say`, which is unchanged (chronicle, soak keys). Talk uses its own `chatRng` and `RelPeek` (never `RelOf`, which fixes a relation), so the Messenger never changes what happens in the town.
- **Status:** sisters Busy at war (ours or hers), Away in a blight, Blocked at relation -0.45 or below, else Online; notables Busy while their trouble is on, else Away; me Busy in war, fire or illness, Away at night. Personal messages per rule (`Mottos`) plus attitude, or the current trouble.
- **Toasts:** `Say` queues a crier toast; a chat line in the same tick replaces it (`Pop`). Up to three rise from the bottom right of the town picture, stay 6 s, sink away; click one to open its chat. Urgent ones (war declared on us, plague, a great fire) also set `NudgeTick`, which shakes the panel for 0.6 s.
- **Emoticons:** codes such as `:)` `(L)` `(bah)` written as separate words are drawn from `resources/emoticons.png` (40 classics, 10x4 cells of 24 px, cut from the owner's reference sheet by `resources/make-emoticons.ps1`, backgrounds made transparent). `Rich` draws and wraps text with them. Never emoji characters.
- **Saved:** the last 40 lines per chat (`Chats` in the save), up to 4 offline and 4 group chats. The Chronicle contact reads `chronicle.txt` as before. Typing delays are not saved.
- Volume (`--soak 60000 --units 1500`): about 3 chat lines a town day (one every 2 minutes), plus toasts for every crier line. Test flag: `--chat` (a screenshot with conversations filled in); `--soak` appends a messenger section.

## Icon
- `resources/make-icon.mjs` draws the icon (16x16 pixel art, variant A: house with a red and a blue dot-man on the road, on a rounded meadow-green tile) and writes `dotburg.ico` (16 to 256), `dotburg.png` (256) and `dotburg-512.png`. Run `node make-icon.mjs build a` to regenerate (`preview` draws both variants). Used as the exe icon (`ApplicationIcon`), the window and taskbar icon (`SetWindowIcon`), in the town window title bar, and as the mod icon (`../dotburg/.claude-plugin/icon.png`, copied by `sync.ps1`). Windows may cache the old icon on shortcuts for a while.

## Hover
- Resting the mouse on a building outlines it and shows a tooltip (Info.cs: RankAtPixel, InfoFor; drawing in Panels.cs DrawTown/HoverTip). Shows type, residents, who is inside, seat of government, statue, lights, pending upgrade, or construction. Test flag: `--hover <lot rank>`.
